## Course Description

In this course, you will get hands-on experience solving a real-world problem in a team with a community partner. You will participate in iterative agile development throughout the semester, culminating with a team presentation and the handing off of the project to your community partner.

## Course Resources

* There is no textbook for this course.
* We will utilize Brightspace, Gradescope, GitHub, MS Teams, and other online resources as needed.
* We will utilize UVM-approved generative AI.

## Grading

This course will use a point-based (instead of percentage-based) grading system. 

* You will earn points by completing assignments throughout the semester. 
* Assignments will be worth different numbers of points based on the difficulty levels and amounts of time expected to complete them. 
* Some points will be team-based, others will be individual. 
* Some points will be earned in class, others will be earned outside of class time.

Your final grade will be based on the total number of points you earn by the end of the semester on this scale:

### Assignments

Points will be earned throughout the semester based on these expectations:

- Regular SCRUM meetings in your team
- Regular meetings with your community partner
- Regular meetings with your faculty mentor
- Regular changes to your project repository
- Regular submission of team peer evaluations
- A final project repository branch that is well-documented
- A final presentation

### Peer Evaluations (Adapted from SEED)

Due to the group work nature of this course, it is imperative that everyone contributes evenly to the project. One of the methods for assessing your personal contribution is by Peer Evaluations. These evaluations are an opportunity to review the contribution and performance of each of your teammates and yourself. You will regularly receive Peer Evaluation Scores that represent your efforts relative to your peers.
 
The Peer Evaluation will ask two questions about “Contribution” and “Inclusivity”. 

* Contribution addresses the quality, quantity, and timeliness of your participation in the project. 
* Inclusivity addresses how well you cultivated an inclusive and respectful environment, in which every team member’s contributions are welcome. 

Your final grade each semester may be adjusted up or down based on these evaluations. Adjustments are made at the discretion of the instructor based on your evaluations. Your grade adjustment will be given after all assignments are graded and posted in Brightspace.
 
| Peer Evaluation Score | Possible Grade Adjustment |
| --------------------- | ------------------------- |
| <0.5 | Likely failing course |
| 0.5 – 0.75 | 30% to 15% reduction of final grade |
| 0.76 – 0.89 | 14% to 3% reduction of final grade |
| 0.9 – 1.09 | No change in final grade |
| 1.1 – 1.5 | 5% to 10% increase in final grade |
 
Example: A Contribution Score of 0.75 reduces your final grade by 15%. An Inclusivity Score of 1.1 increases your final grade by 5%. Your final grade adjustment would be -10%.
 
It is critical you are honest in these evaluations of both your peers and yourself. Any misrepresentation, false statements, or unjustified evaluations to improve your own personal grade or change a teammate’s grade will be considered a violation of UVM’s Academic Integrity policy.

### Community Partner Evaluations (Adapted from SEED)

As students, you are typically only held accountable to the instructor and their course requirements. In this course you are also being held accountable to your community partner and their expectations. At the end of the semester the community partner will be asked to evaluate their team based on their perceptions of the quality, effectiveness, effort, and methods used to find a solution to their problem. Keep this in mind as you interact with your community partner.

## Self-Regulation (Adapted from SEED)

Because your community partner has high expectations, we as instructors have high expectations, and you have high expectations for yourself, this semester may be more stressful than last semester. The best way to manage stress is to know what you can control and then focus on controlling those things. You can control what you work on, when you work on it, how you present yourself to your team members, clients, and instructors, and what you do to recharge outside of coursework. An appropriate amount of stress stimulates high performance, but an excessively high amount of stress is damaging. If you find yourself under too much stress, remember that you can get support from your team members, instructors, teaching assistants, faculty mentor, and community partner. You need to do the work, but we want you to succeed, and we are here to help you learn and succeed.

## Accommodations

In keeping with University policy, any student with a documented disability interested in utilizing ADA accommodations should contact Student Accessibility Services (SAS), the office of Disability Services on campus for students. SAS works with students and faculty in an interactive process to explore reasonable and appropriate accommodations, which are communicated to faculty in an accommodation letter. All students are strongly recommended to discuss with their faculty the accommodations they plan to use in each course. Faculty who receive Letters of Accommodation with Disability Related Flexible accommodations will need to fill out the Disability Related Flexibility Agreement. Any questions from faculty or students on the agreement should be directed to the SAS specialist who is indicated on the letter.

## Religious Accommodations

Students have the right to practice the religion of their choice. Each semester, students must submit in writing to their instructors as early as possible, and at least one week prior to their religious holiday or observance, the date(s) of the conflict or absence. Faculty must permit students who provide appropriate notice of the need to miss a class, assignment, or exam for religious observance with the ability to obtain information covered in class and reschedule or complete an alternative to the applicable assignment or exam date. Absences for religious observance shall be without penalty, however such absences may not fundamentally alter course requirements.

## Student Athletes

In order to be excused from classes, student athletes should submit appropriate documentation to the Professor in advance of all scheduling conflicts within the first two weeks of class. Those missing class are expected to submit make-up assignments within a reasonable time period.
