# CS 3920 Discussion Format

Once you hava a team and a discussion topic, here's the timeline before the discussion class:

* The instructor will email the team at the beginning of the week before your scheduled discussion day. This email will include links to articles that were sent to the class in previous semesters, which you will want to read through. These links may be stale or you may find articles you like better. If you switch out any articles, make sure the new ones don't have a paywall (New York Times is okay because UVM has a subscription for students). If you find new links, share them with your team for approval.
* Meet with the instructor during the last five minutes of the class period the week before your discussion. This is when the list of readings should be finalized so that the instructor can release them to the class on Brightspace. This is also when the instructor can answer any questions you have about leading your discussion topic.
* Collaborate with your team to prepare for your discussion day. You will need to submit your presentation slides on Gradescope. One person can submit for the team, then they can add the rest of the team members to the submission.

## Requirements for the Discussion

Here are the requirements of the discussion class period:

* All team members need to take a turn leading the class.
	* "Leading" could mean talking through presentation slides, guiding the class in an activity, asking the class questions and fielding answers, etc.
* You need to build in **at least two** interactive elements, which can include but aren't limited to:
  * **Think-Pair-Share**: Pose a question to the class in a presentation slide and give them 30 seconds to think about the answer silently, 3-5 minutes to talk with the people near them, then ask at least 3 people to share out answers.
  	* Example question: "Can AI be sustainable and even have a positive impact? In what way(s)?"
  * **Four Corners**: Label the four corners of the room A, B, C, and D. Then post a multiple choice question on the projector and ask students to go to the corner of the room representing the answer they would choose to the question. Have students talk for 3-5 minutes with the other people who chose the same answer, then ask one representative from each corner of the room to share out the group's thoughts.
  	* Example question: "The negative impacts of social media on youth outweigh the positives. A) Agree, B) Partly Agree, C) Partly Disagree, D) Disagree"
  * **Divide-and-Conquer**: Divide the class into groups, assigning different positions to each group. Give them a task to complete within their groups for 5 minutes, then have a representative from each group share out.
  	* Example: Ask students to find local jobs they can apply to, but half the class can use the internet and the other half cannot (you give that half newspapers to look through).
* Your discussion will have a hard stop five minutes before the end of class time so that the instructor can meet with next week's team. You may want to plan for 40 minutes and give yourself 5 minutes of flex time. 
  * It's good practice to write notes in your presentation slides and/or use your phone's timer during the discussion to keep yourself at a good pace.
