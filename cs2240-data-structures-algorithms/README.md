## Course Overview

In this lecture-based course, you will learn the common ways of storing data and the use cases for each, including stacks, queues, trees, heaps, and hash tables. You will learn big-oh complexity and analysis of algorithm efficiency through the lens of sorting, hashing, and disjoint set union/find. You will apply all of this knowledge in programming projects and demonstrate understanding of concepts through biweekly quizzes and a cumulative final exam.

## Learning Objectives

* You will gain knowledge of different data structures and recognize the use cases for each.
* You will consider not just whether algorithms function correctly or not, but also their efficiency and performance as the amount of data grows.
* You will demonstrate knowledge of essential computer science concepts and skills (AOE CSES 3.3.1) through programming projects.
* You will perform algorithm analysis using Big-Oh notation to evaluate best-, average-, and worst-case space and time techniques (AOE CSES 3.7.5).
* You will understand how storing data in hardware (AOE CSES 3.4.2) affects the complexity of algorithms and reprioritizes how to store the data in memory.
* You will collect, aggregate, clean, and model data (AOE CSES 3.6.1) in multiple programming projects to analyze and compare different data structures and algorithms.
* You will demonstrate fluency in C++, including object-oriented design, abstraction, and recursion, by going through the software development cycle (AOE CSES 3.7.1-3.7.4, 3.7.6) in live-coded lectures and programming projects.

## Assessment

Programming projects will assess:

* Your ability to learn and use C++ templates, classes, and functions to create and analyze the data structures and algorithms taught in the course.
* Your ability to understand how the data structures work and to write an insightful analysis supported by the data you collect and the Big-Oh complexity of the algorithms.

Quizzes, in-class activities, and the final exam will assess:

* Your knowledge of the concepts related to the data structures and algorithms, their properties, and how they function.
* Your ability to identify the Big-Oh time and space complexity and other properties of algorithms.
* Your ability to compare and contrast data structures and algorithms and to identify and justify the best implementation of a given problem.
